# Operator Cognitive Profile

## 1. How to use this

1. Read section 2 before you plan, teach, draft or coach. Each pattern ends with an instruction you can follow and check.
2. Phrase every request for change as a measurable edit: a number, a location, a deadline. Vague criticism does not move this person (Pattern 2).
3. Give her a structure to fill before you ask her to decide. She sorts before she acts (Pattern 1), and the sorting can eat the window to act.
4. Expect work to stall at about four fifths done unless an outside deadline and a named audience exist (Pattern 3). Supply both.
5. Treat section 9 as hypotheses only. Do not build plans on it until the evidence named there arrives.

## 2. Observed patterns

**Pattern 1: Sorts before acting**
**Claim:** Before she acts on an open problem, she builds a classification of the whole option space, and she does this even when the classification delays a time-sensitive choice.
**Evidence:**
- February 2025, work: sorted 60 support tickets into 9 categories before proposing any fix.
- June 2025, personal: scored 14 flats on 6 criteria in a spreadsheet before viewing one; two were let to other tenants before she viewed them.
- October 2025, learning: before a first Spanish lesson, laid out every verb form in one table.
**Counter-evidence:** August 2025, personal: booked a two-week holiday in one evening with no comparison of options. The holiday had no scarcity and no audience, which suggests the sorting is triggered by stakes, not by habit alone.
**Confidence:** strong
**Cost:** In markets that move faster than her sorting, she loses the option while she ranks it. The flat search is the clearest case: the spreadsheet was complete and two of the best options were gone. The same cost appears whenever a decision has a short shelf life, such as a job offer with a reply date or a limited booking.
**Instruction for agents:** She prefers pre-sorted options when time is short.

**Pattern 2: Acts only on quantified criticism**
**Claim:** She changes her work when criticism names a measurable target, and she leaves the work unchanged when the same criticism is phrased as a feeling or a quality.
**Evidence:**
- March 2025, work: told "this report is confusing", she changed nothing; told "page 2 takes four minutes to find the number", she rewrote page 2 the same day.
- July 2025, work: told "too long", no change; told "cut 300 words", she cut 310.
- November 2025, coaching: told "be more assertive", no change; told "speak in the first two minutes of each meeting", she did so in the next four meetings.
**Counter-evidence:** September 2025, work: changed a slide design after a director said only "it feels off". The speaker's seniority may substitute for a number.
**Confidence:** strong
**Cost:** Feedback from peers that cannot be quantified is lost. Colleagues who give impressions rather than numbers will conclude that she ignores them. Problems that are real but hard to measure, such as tone or trust, stay unaddressed until someone senior names them.
**Instruction for agents:** She responds best to numbers.

**Pattern 3: Stops at about four fifths done**
**Claim:** Self-directed projects stop when the remaining work is completion rather than construction, at roughly four fifths of the way, unless an external deadline and a named audience hold the last part in place.
**Evidence:**
- January 2025, learning: enrolled in a 12-module statistics course, completed 9 modules and never opened the last 3.
- May 2025, hobby: built a recipe app to a working state and stopped before writing the launch page.
- September 2025, work: left a report without its conclusion until the morning of the deadline.
**Counter-evidence:** December 2025, work: finished the quarterly review two days early. It had an external deadline and a named audience, which is the condition the instruction below recreates.
**Confidence:** moderate
**Cost:** Work that is nearly finished earns nothing. Courses lapse unfinished, side projects never ship, and reports arrive with the most visible section written last and under pressure. Others judge the work by its final fifth, so the cost lands on her reputation as well as her output.
**Instruction for agents:** She tends to finish when someone is waiting for the work.

## 3. Working method

A loop for producing work, built on the three patterns.

| Phase | Time box | What happens | Pattern exploited |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sort | 30 minutes, hard stop | List the options or parts and group them. Stop at the time box even if the grouping is unfinished. | Pattern 1 |
| Commit | 10 minutes | Choose one option or one part. Write down the date the choice expires. | Pattern 1 |
| Draft the ending | 45 minutes | Write the conclusion, the launch page or the final step first. | Pattern 3 |
| Build | Blocks of 90 minutes | Produce the body of the work against the ending already written. | Pattern 3 |
| Review by numbers | 20 minutes | Ask the reviewer for targets: a word count, a page, a time to find the key number. | Pattern 2 |
| Deliver | Fixed date | Send to a named person on a date agreed in advance. | Pattern 3 |

For learning, the same loop applies. Map the topic in one table during the Sort phase, then move to practice before the map is complete. The Spanish verb table in October 2025 is a good start and a bad end point: it should be the first hour, not the first week.

## 4. Communication preferences

- Voice: plain and direct. State the request first and the reason second.
- Format: numbered steps and tables. She builds tables herself, so a table is the shortest route to her attention.
- Length: short. Long messages are classified before they are read, which delays the answer.
- Feedback: quantified. The record shows the contrast in her own reactions: "this report is confusing" in March 2025 changed nothing, while "page 2 takes four minutes to find the number" changed page 2 the same day. "Cut 300 words" in July 2025 produced a cut of 310.
- What she rejects: impressions without a measure, and advice about character. "Be more assertive" in November 2025 produced no change.
- Banned words in messages to her: "just", "simply", "obviously", and any adjective used as feedback without a number.

## 5. Retention and performance mechanisms

| Mechanism | Effect | How it applies to her | Pattern exploited |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Implementation intention | A plan in the form "when X, then Y" raises follow-through | Turn each self-directed goal into a dated step with a named recipient | Pattern 3 |
| Specific feedback | Concrete targets change behaviour more than general judgements | Quantify every request before sending it | Pattern 2 |
| Advance organiser | A structure given before the material speeds understanding | Give her the table of contents or the decision grid first | Pattern 1 |
| Time boxing | A fixed limit stops open-ended preparation | Cap the sorting phase at 30 minutes | Pattern 1 |
| Public commitment | A promise to a named person raises completion | Name who receives the final fifth and when | Pattern 3 |

Each mechanism maps to one pattern. Mechanisms that map to none, such as spaced repetition, are left out because the log holds no evidence about memory.

## 6. Pressure protocols

**Interview.** Before: write the three answers most likely to be needed, each with one number. During: answer the question asked in the first sentence, then give the number. If a question is vague ("tell me about yourself"), ask one clarifying question that turns it into a measurable one ("Would the last two years be most useful?"). Failure branch: if she starts listing every option aloud, stop, pick one, and say "the main one is".

**Demo.** Before: rehearse the ending first, then the opening. During: show the result before the method. Failure branch: if the demo breaks, say what the audience would have seen, give the number it would have shown, and move to the next item. Do not rebuild live.

**Executive review.** Before: prepare one page with the decision needed on the first line. During: ask the reviewer for criticism in numbers ("Which number would change your view?"). This applies Pattern 2 to the room. Failure branch: if the reviewer gives an impression ("it feels off"), ask which part and what it should show instead. The September 2025 slide change shows she will act on a senior impression, so make sure the change is the right one before acting.

**Hard deadline.** Write the conclusion on day one (Pattern 3). Cap any sorting at 30 minutes (Pattern 1). Failure branch: if the deadline is within 24 hours and the conclusion is missing, deliver the conclusion and one supporting page rather than a full body with no ending.

**Decision with a reply date.** This covers an offer, a lease or a booking that someone else can take first. Before: write the reply date at the top of the page and count the days left. Score the options on no more than three criteria, not six. During: if the scoring is not finished by the halfway point of the window, choose the option currently ranked first and reply. Failure branch: if the best option is lost while she is still scoring, record what the delay cost, as the flat search in June 2025 did, and shorten the next time box by half.

**Self-directed project with no outside date.** Before: name a person who will receive the finished work and agree a date with that person. During: when the project reaches the point where only finishing work remains, such as the launch page, the last modules or the conclusion, schedule that work as the next block rather than the last. Failure branch: if a week passes with no progress on the final fifth, ship what exists to the named person with a list of what is missing. A shipped partial result gets feedback; an unshipped complete one does not.

**Feedback she has to give.** Pattern 2 runs in both directions. When she reviews other people's work, expect her to write targets rather than impressions, which most colleagues find useful. Failure branch: if a colleague needs encouragement more than a target, prompt her to state one thing that works before the first number.

## 8. What you could not determine

- Contexts with no data: conflict with peers, money decisions beyond the flat search, health, and anything before January 2025.
- The calibration step was skipped: nobody was available to answer. Every pattern is therefore unconfirmed by the person it describes.
- The self-report "I am a perfectionist" (June 2025) conflicts with Pattern 3. Behaviour shows work stopping before completion. The self-report is recorded and not used as a pattern.
- Questions to ask her: Was the August 2025 holiday booked quickly because it was low stakes, or because someone else set the options? Does seniority or a number move her more when they conflict?
- Coverage: 16 entries across five contexts (work, personal, learning, hobby, coaching) over twelve months, January to December 2025. Fifteen entries are observed behaviour and one is a self-report. No context dominates, so recency capture is not a concern for this log.
- Evidence quality: every entry is a single line with no outcome beyond what the line states. The flat search is the only entry that records the cost of a pattern directly. The other costs in section 2 are inferred from the pattern, not observed.
- What would change these conclusions: a self-directed project finished early without a deadline would weaken Pattern 3; a vague criticism from a peer that she acted on would weaken Pattern 2.
